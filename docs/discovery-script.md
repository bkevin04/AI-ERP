# Discovery script and outreach

## Who to talk to

Operations managers, customer service managers, inside sales managers and owners at companies with 10 to 100 employees in:

- A. Specialty wholesale distribution (industrial consumables, packaging, medical and dental, salon and beauty, electronics components, pet, specialty chemicals)
- B. Importers and own-brand wholesalers selling to retailers
- C. Small make-to-order manufacturers and job shops

Fifteen interviews total, at least four per segment. Decide the segment by day 30.

## Outreach email (cold, under 90 words)

Subject: how do orders reach you?

Hi {first name},

I'm building software that turns emailed and PDF purchase orders into clean sales orders, with a person approving before anything is booked. Before building more, I'm talking to people who actually handle inbound orders at {company type} companies.

Could I get twenty minutes to hear how the last five orders that arrived by email were handled? No pitch, I'm after the steps and the time they take.

If useful, I'll share a short write-up of what I learn across companies like yours.

{name}

## Interview script (20 minutes)

1. Walk me through what happened to the last five orders that arrived by email. Who touched them, in which system, in what order?
2. How long does one order take from inbox to booked? What makes a slow one slow?
3. What goes wrong? Wrong product, wrong quantity, wrong price, wrong ship-to, duplicate? How often? Who notices and when?
4. Which systems hold customers, products, prices and orders today? Who keeps them up to date?
5. What tools have you tried for this? Why did they stick or not?
6. If a draft order appeared in your system five minutes after the email, with the lines highlighted that need a human look, what would still worry you?
7. What other repetitive tasks eat the same team's week? List everything, even small ones.
8. Could you share ten anonymised sample orders (email bodies, PDFs, spreadsheets) so I can test against real documents?

Record: minutes per order, orders per week, error types and frequency, systems, tools tried, the long-tail task list, willingness to pilot.

## Pilot offer

Three free pilots for eight weeks in exchange for:

- weekly 20-minute feedback call
- anonymised sample documents up front
- a testimonial if it works

Success metric agreed up front: minutes per order before and after, exceptions per 100 orders.

## Segment decision criteria

- minutes per week on the wedge task per company
- willingness to pilot free
- budget owner reachable
- the task is identical across companies in the segment
- existing tools were tried and rejected
